Answers to the questions newcomers ask, and ten tips for your first game.

## Questions

### Is it free?

Yes. There is nothing to buy, no account and no advertising. The game and its source are in a public repository.

### Do I need to install anything?

No. [Open the game](https://colebrumley.github.io/new-world/) in the latest Chrome, Firefox or Safari and it plays in the page. After the first visit it also starts with no connection, and your browser can install it as an app if you like. See [[Home]].

### Does it save my game?

Yes, by itself: with the Autosave option on (it is on at first), the game keeps itself in your browser after every action, and **Load Game** on the title screen picks it up again. You also have eight slots of your own and can export a game to a file. Saves live in the browser you play in, so clearing that browser's data for the site deletes them; export a file to be safe. See [[Help and Options]].

### Can I play on a phone?

Not well. The game is made for a window at least 1024 by 640 and for a keyboard and mouse or trackpad. It understands touch for scrolling and pinch-zooming the map, so a large tablet held sideways may do, but a phone screen is too small for the colony and Europe screens.

### How long is a game?

The game starts in 1492. There is a turn for each year until 1600, then two a year, spring and autumn. If you have not declared independence by 1800 the game ends there and is scored: about five hundred turns in all. A war of independence can run on to 1850. You can save and stop whenever you like, or retire early (Shift-R) to have the game scored as it stands. See [[Score and Hall of Fame]].

### How do I win?

Every game ends with a score, and the best go into the Hall of Fame. The score counts your colonists (specialists most), your Founding Fathers, your gold and how strongly your people support independence. The real victory is to declare independence and win the war that follows: that adds points and multiplies the whole score, more if you are the first power to do it. See [[The Crown and Independence]] and [[Score and Hall of Fame]].

### How is a game lost?

Three ways. If you hold no colony in 1600 the Crown withdraws your charter. If you declare independence and the King's army takes enough of your colonies and ports, the rebellion is put down. And a war of independence not won by 1850 ends in a peace on the Crown's terms. Reaching 1800 without declaring is not a loss: the game is scored, and you may play on. Every ending leads to an end page with the ways on from there: a new game, a saved one, or a last look over the map. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

### How do I make money early?

Sell what your colonies make in Europe: furs, sugar, tobacco, cotton and lumber to begin with, and the cloth, rum, cigars and coats your workshops make from them, which fetch more. Explore: ruins and rumors of lost cities can pay in gold, and a scout who speaks with a native chief may be given some. Trade with the natives, who pay well for goods they lack. See [[Europe and Trade]] and [[Native Peoples]].

### Why is my colony starving?

Every colonist in a colony eats two food a turn. If the colony grows less than it eats, the store runs down, and when it is empty a colonist dies. Put more people to farming or fishing, choose squares that grow food, and have a pioneer plow them. Ocean squares give fish only once the colony has built Docks. The colony screen's People panel shows food made, eaten and stored. See [[Colonies]].

### Why did my price fall?

Every sale in Europe pushes that good's price down a little, and every purchase pushes it up. The other powers trade in the same market, so their sales lower your prices too. Prices drift back over time. Sell several kinds of goods rather than flooding one, and look at the two prices on the Europe screen before you sell. The Dutch market falls more slowly. See [[Europe and Trade]].

### What do the natives want?

Goods they lack, room to live, and to be left in peace. A chief will tell a scout which goods his people want, and they pay well for them. They grow alarmed when colonies crowd their land and soldiers gather near them, and when colonists take their land without paying. Missions, gifts and fair trade calm them. See [[Native Peoples]].

### When should I declare independence?

You can once half your colonists support it (50% rebel sentiment). Before you do, make sure the colonies that will be attacked are well defended, with veteran soldiers, artillery and stout walls, and that the Sons of Liberty are strong in them. When the war starts, each colony at 50% membership or more turns some of its veteran soldiers and dragoons into Continental Army: more as membership climbs toward 100%, up to half the colony's population, and always at least one. The Continental Congress report (F3) shows the force the King will send. Declaring cuts you off from Europe, so stock up on muskets, horses and tools first. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

### Where do I report a bug?

Open an issue in the repository's [Issues](https://github.com/colebrumley/new-world/issues). Say what you did, what you expected and what happened. An exported save (see [[Help and Options]]) helps a great deal.

## Ten tips for your first game

1. **Found your first colony on the coast.** Only a colony beside the sea can load and unload ships, and your trade with Europe goes through it. The game warns you before you found a colony with no access to the ocean.
2. **Grow more food than you eat.** Each colonist eats two food a turn. A colony that stores 200 food turns it into a new free colonist, so surplus food is free people.
3. **Keep the pioneer busy.** Plowed land grows more food and more of every crop, and a road raises the lumber, furs, ore and silver a square gives. Each job uses 20 tools, so buy more in Europe before the pioneer runs dry.
4. **Spread your sales.** Each cargo you sell lowers that good's price. Selling a little of several goods earns more than dumping a great deal of one, and goods made in your workshops sell for more than the raw materials.
5. **Put a statesman in the Town Hall early.** His liberty bells raise Sons of Liberty membership, which first removes the Tory penalty and then adds a production bonus for every worker in the colony. The same bells bring Founding Fathers to your Congress.
6. **Explore with a scout.** Mount a colonist on 50 horses. A scout gets better results from lost city rumors, and a chief he visits may give him gold, tell him of the country around, or make him a seasoned scout.
7. **Let the natives teach you.** Walk a free colonist or an indentured servant into a friendly settlement and ask to be taught. Most settlements teach their trade once, for nothing, and an expert farmer or fisherman brings in more than a plain colonist.
8. **Mind the natives' land.** Colonies close to a settlement alarm its people, and working their land without paying alarms them more. Pay for land when you can, keep a little distance, and send missionaries.
9. **Fortify a soldier in each colony.** From the turn after he digs in, a fortified unit defends half again as well, against raiders, rivals and in the end the Crown.
10. **Prepare before you declare.** Once you declare, Europe is closed to you. Build up muskets, horses and tools, raise Sons of Liberty membership past 50% in your strongest colonies, and keep veteran soldiers there: on the first turn of the war some of them join the Continental Army, more the higher the membership.
