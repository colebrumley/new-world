The game's own words, in alphabetical order, each with the page that explains it.

## A to C

**Alarm.** How uneasy a native people is about you, from calm to hostile. Colonies close by, soldiers near their settlements and land taken without paying raise it; trade, gifts and missions lower it. A people whose alarm runs high may go to war. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Artillery.** A heavy gun, bought in Europe or built in a colony. Strong in attack and in a colony's defence, weak in the open. See [[Units]].

**Bells.** Short for liberty bells, made by statesmen in a colony's Town Hall. They raise the colony's Sons of Liberty membership and bring Founding Fathers to your Congress. See [[Colonies]] and [[Founding Fathers]].

**Boycott.** When the Crown raises your tax you may refuse it by throwing a cargo into the harbour. That good can then be neither bought nor sold in Europe until you pay the back taxes. See [[Europe and Trade]].

**Brave.** A warrior of a native people. Braves visit and trade, and in a war they raid your colonies. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Capital.** The chief settlement of a native people. It is larger than the others, and what you do there counts for more, for better and worse. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Charter.** Your royal licence to colonize. A power that holds no colony in 1600 loses it, and the game. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

**Colonist.** Any of your people who can found a colony, work in one or take up a role such as soldier, pioneer or scout. See [[Units]].

**Continental Army.** The rebel army. When you declare independence, veterans in colonies with strong Sons of Liberty membership join it, and it fights better than ordinary soldiers. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

**Continental Congress.** The assembly your bells fill with Founding Fathers. Its report (F3) shows who has joined, who is next, and the force the King holds ready. See [[Founding Fathers]].

**Convert.** A native person who has come to live among your colonists, usually thanks to a mission. A convert works fields and the sea better than an ordinary colonist but cannot be schooled in a trade. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Crosses.** Religious feeling, made by a colony's church and its preachers. When enough have gathered, a new colonist comes forward on the docks in Europe. See [[Europe and Trade]].

**The Crown's expeditionary force.** The King's army and fleet held ready to put down a rebellion: regulars, cavalry, artillery and men-of-war. It grows through the game as taxes fill the royal purse, and it lands when you declare independence. Also called the Royal Expeditionary Force. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

**Custom House.** A colony building that sells chosen goods to Europe for you, without a ship. It goes on selling during a boycott, and after independence. See [[Buildings]].

## D to H

**Declaration of Independence.** Your break with the Crown, possible once half your colonists support it. It cannot be undone, and it starts the War of Independence. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

**Docks.** In Europe, where new colonists wait for a ship to the New World. In a colony, the building that lets colonists fish the ocean squares. See [[Europe and Trade]] and [[Buildings]].

**Dragoon.** A colonist with both muskets and horses: the strongest of your ordinary land units. See [[Units]].

**Expert.** A colonist trained in a trade, such as an expert farmer or a master carpenter. Experts do their own work much better than a free colonist. Also called a specialist. See [[Units]].

**Fortify.** An order that has a unit dig in where it stands. From the next turn it defends half again as well. See [[Combat]].

**Founding Father.** A notable figure who joins your Congress when enough bells are rung, each with a lasting benefit. There are twenty-five. See [[Founding Fathers]].

**Free colonist.** An ordinary colonist with no special trade. Can learn one at school or from the natives. See [[Units]].

**Go to.** An order (G) that sends a unit to a colony, to Europe or to a square of your choosing, over as many turns as it takes. See [[Controls]].

**Hall of Fame.** The ten best finished games in your browser, best rating first. See [[Score and Hall of Fame]].

**Hammers.** What carpenters make from lumber, and what a colony spends on whatever it is building. Hammers cannot be moved or sold. See [[Buildings]].

**Hold.** The cargo space of a ship or wagon train. One hold carries up to 100 of a single good; a full hold is a full load. See [[Europe and Trade]].

## I to R

**Indentured servant.** A colonist bound to work off his passage. Good in the fields, poor at skilled work in the buildings. See [[Units]].

**Lost City Rumor.** A mark on the map where something is said to lie. Entering the square may bring gold, treasure, new colonists or nothing, and now and then the party is lost. Scouts do best. See [[The Map]].

**Lot.** An amount of a good less than a full hold, for when the treasury will not stretch to 100. See [[Europe and Trade]].

**Man-of-War.** The Crown's great warship. It carries the expeditionary force's troops across the ocean. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

**Mission.** A church in a native settlement, founded by a missionary. It calms the settlement and may bring converts to your colonies. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Native land.** Land that a native people regards as theirs. Working it may mean paying for it or angering them. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Petty criminal.** The least skilled of colonists, good only for plain field work until schooled. See [[Units]].

**Pioneer.** A colonist with tools, who clears forest, plows fields and builds roads. Each job uses 20 tools. See [[Units]].

**Privateer.** A fast warship for attacking other powers' merchant ships. See [[Combat]].

**Rating.** Your Colonial Rating: the score weighted by difficulty, as a percentage, with the honour it earns. See [[Score and Hall of Fame]].

**Rebel sentiment.** The share of all your colonists who support independence, worked out from each colony's Sons of Liberty membership. You may declare at 50%. See [[The Crown and Independence]].

## S to Z

**Scout.** A colonist on horseback, the best explorer. Scouts speak with native chiefs and do best at rumors. A seasoned scout does better still. See [[Units]].

**Sea Lane.** The open water down the east and west edges of the map. From there a ship can sail to Europe. See [[The Map]].

**Sentry.** An order that leaves a unit out of the turn's round of orders until a stranger comes near or its ship reaches a colony. See [[Controls]].

**Settlement.** A native village, camp or city. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Sons of Liberty.** The colonists of a colony who support independence, shown as a percentage on the colony screen. At 50% and at 100% they raise every worker's output. See [[Colonies]].

**Specialist.** See Expert.

**Stockade.** A colony's first wall. It strengthens defence, but a colony with a stockade can no longer be left with fewer than three colonists. See [[Buildings]].

**Tax.** The Crown's share of every sale in Europe. It rises from time to time through the game, and is never paid after independence. See [[Europe and Trade]].

**Tory.** A colonist loyal to the Crown: everyone in a colony who is not a Son of Liberty. Too many Tories slow every worker in a colony. See [[Colonies]].

**Trade route.** A list of colonies, and Europe for a ship, with what to load and unload at each. A ship or wagon train on a route runs it turn after turn by itself. See [[Colonies]].

**Treasure train.** Gold found in a Lost City Rumour, in burial mounds, or among the ruins of a destroyed native settlement. It fills six holds, so only a Galleon can carry one. As the game stands, nothing pays a treasure out: neither your galleon nor the Crown can cash one yet. See [[Units]].

**Tribute.** Goods demanded from a native settlement by an armed unit. It may be paid, refused or laughed at, and it raises their alarm. See [[Native Peoples]].

**Veteran.** A soldier hardened by battle, or trained for it, who fights half again as well. A colonist who wins a fight may be promoted to veteran. See [[Units]].

**View mode.** A way to move a cursor over the map without moving a unit (V). See [[Controls]].

**Wagon train.** A land carrier for goods between colonies, built in a colony. See [[Units]].

**Warehouse.** A colony's store of goods. It holds 100 of each good; a Warehouse building raises that. Food is not limited. See [[Colonies]].
