# Visual checklist (R-1006)

What the map must look like, and what checks each point. The baselines are taken at 1280 x 800
by `test/e2e/visual.spec.ts`, which also writes a PNG of each view to `test-results/` to be
looked at when the art is changed. The views of the unexplored map as first drawn are kept in
`docs/chart/`.

The baselines do not depend on the platform. The map is drawn only from palette-indexed art
scaled by whole numbers, so what it must show can be computed without a browser:
`test/helpers/reference-canvas.ts` runs the game's own `render()` against a plain pixel buffer
(and refuses any colour outside the palette, any art off the pixel grid, and any smoothing). The
e2e test requires the browser's canvas to match that reference pixel for pixel at every zoom, and
the fingerprint kept as the baseline is the reference's. `test/unit/ui/render.test.ts` pins the
same renderer in the unit suite.

| # | Point | Checked by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everything drawn in code is in one palette of 32 colours (`PALETTE` in `src/ui/pixel-art.ts`; the values and what each is for are in `docs/ART.md`). Every pixel on the canvas is a colour of that palette or of the baked pictures' own sets, which together are `ART_COLORS`, at most 255. | unit: the palette's 32 entries are distinct and pinned by value; `render.ts`, `tiles.ts`, `minimap.ts` contain no colour literals; `ART_COLORS` fits in a byte; every baked floor's commonest colour is its terrain's ground colour, and water is never the flat colour of any land. The reference canvas refuses a fill colour outside `PALETTE`. e2e: every canvas pixel at every zoom is a colour of `ART_COLORS`. |
| 2 | One pixel grid: art is 16 x 16 to the square; squares are 64, 32, 16 or 8 pixels, so an art pixel is 4, 2, 1 or (halved art) 1 pixel. No smoothing. | unit: `tileSizeFor` gives multiples of 8; art is 16 x 16. e2e: no off-palette (blended) pixels. |
| 3 | Every terrain can be told apart, open from wooded, hills and mountains by their shapes; rivers, roads, plowed fields, resources and rumors show. The ground and what stands on it are baked pictures on the 32 grid and smaller copies of the same pictures, in the same colours, on the 16 grid (R-1042): each ground textured in its own palette colour, each of the eight forests a canopy that fills its square and is told by its trees (spruce, pine, pine among round crowns, round crowns with autumn orange, palms, cypress in water, jungle, thorny bushes). A river is one of sixteen pieces by the sides it runs on to (`riverMask`), which are five shapes turned: an end, a straight, a bend, a tee and a cross, the cross also where it runs on to no side (R-1047). A neighbour the viewer has not seen continues nothing, so the piece tells nothing of the square beyond it; a river left with no side to run on to that way is a pool alone. It is drawn for its grid: a body of `blue` three art pixels wide for a minor river and five for a major one on the 32 grid, two and three on the 16, crossing each side on exactly the middle cells of that side so that two squares meet cell for cell; each arm winds a little to the right of the way in (the variant mirrors it), so opposite arms make an S; a bend, a tee and a cross meet in a disc a cell wider than the body, and an end has a pool there; every other cell along both banks is `brightBlue`, a broken pale rim that never touches itself, lies on no body cell and keeps off the outermost cells of the square. Through a forest it runs among the trees: the canopy, joins and all, is cut back from the far (north) bank alone, two art pixels straight above the water on the 32 grid and one on the 16, the ground showing there and the trees above the cut shaded `deepForest`, while the trees on the near bank and to either side stand right up to the water, which makes the far bank read as behind it; over hills and mountains it is simply laid on top. At the farthest zoom a river is a line one cell wide from the middle cell to the middle of each side, with no rim. A special resource is marked by a picture of the thing itself in the middle of its square, three quarters of the square across with a dark outline (24 art pixels on the 32 grid, a 12-pixel copy on the 16 grid, nothing at the farthest zoom), drawn over the forest and under the road. A Lost City Rumor is a round gold seal (`rumorSealArt`: yellow shaded orange, an ink rim, a white glint, a stepped pyramid in wood) filling the lower left quarter of the square on the 32 and 16 grids and a gold dot on the 8; it stands over the resource marker's corner and under a road, and the colony view's totem keeps to the upper left quarter. | unit: all terrain art distinct; features change the art; the river's pieces (the cells on each side, the quarter turns, the mirrored variant, the S, the rim, the pool, the line at the farthest zoom); thirteen distinct markers at both sizes, each within the middle of the square, over the trees and under the road; the seal's inks, quarter and roundness at each grid, whole over every resource's marker, clear of the totem. e2e sheet baseline. |
| 4 | Terrain is readable at every zoom; at the smallest each terrain is one flat colour and forest is never the colour of its open ground. | unit: mini art; e2e: at least 8 colours on screen at zoom 0, 12 at the others. |
| 5 | Each unit type has its own figure, in a box of its owner's colour: English red, French blue, Spanish yellow, Dutch orange, the Crown white, each native people its own. | unit: 24 distinct pieces; colour follows the owner. e2e: orders box test, sheet baseline. |
| 6 | European units carry an orders plate at the top left in the owner's colour with the order letter (a count of filled holds on a foreign ship); native bands carry none. A unit standing on others shows a shadow behind it. | unit: plate, label, stack, no plate for natives. |
| 7 | A colony is a house under its owner's roof with the population on a dark plate: white under half Sons of Liberty, green from half, blue at all. | unit: colony art by colour, number and band. |
| 8 | A settlement is a tent, a longhouse or a pyramid by its people's advancement, in their colour; a capital flies a pennant; an exclamation mark at the top right shows the mood from green (content) to red (hostile). | unit: shapes, pennant, mark and its colours. |
| 9 | The unit awaiting orders has a white frame that blinks. | e2e: the canvas changes over time; `?still` freezes it. |
| 10 | The sea shimmers when the Water shimmer option is on; a unit of ours slides to the next square unless Fast piece slide is on. | e2e: animation redraws counted in `data-ticks`, none with `?still`. |
| 11 | The minimap uses the flat terrain colours, bare vellum for what is unexplored and the page's dark beyond the map, and marks the view with a white box lined in ink. | e2e `map.spec.ts`. |
| 12 | The unexplored map is an explorer's chart: a vellum floor at each grid (32, 16 and 8 art pixels to the square) in place of the dark, hatched in ink along every edge that meets an explored square, with a compass rose four squares across (at most one to a 24 x 24 region), sea serpents and ships standing where every square they cover is still unknown, placed by the map's seed. All of it is in six of the 32 palette colours (parchment, sand, hill, earth, wood, ink), baked from `art/chart/`; beyond the map's edge the canvas stays the page's dark. | unit: `chart.test.ts` (placement), `pixel-art.test.ts` (inks, hatching by edge mask, marks at each grid), `render.test.ts` (vellum, hatching on the vellum side only, a mark goes when one of its squares is known; chart baseline). e2e: `visual.spec.ts` chart baselines at every zoom (`test-results/visual-chart-zoom*.png`), `map.spec.ts` minimap, `shell.spec.ts`. |
| 13 | Squares do not meet as hard tiles (R-1041). Above the smallest zoom a square is drawn in layers: its floor (`floorArt`), then a little of each orthogonal neighbour's floor where that neighbour's open ground differs, stamped through an uneven band along that side (`EDGE_MASKS`, `blendArt`; north, east, south, west, later over earlier), then whatever stands on it (`overArt`). Both squares spill across an edge, with no order between terrains; a forest counts as the open ground of its row; ocean and sea lane never blend. The coast is carried by the water square alone: a water square with land among its eight neighbours is painted as the land beside it and its water laid back in four quarters, each one of eight shapes chosen by the two sides and the corner that bound it (`COAST_MASKS`, `coastArt`), with a line of `wave` where water meets land: the shallows. Water never spills onto land: a land square's water side shows the land that water square is painted as. A neighbour that is off the map or unexplored shows nothing of itself. The smallest zoom, the minimap and the colony screen draw whole flat squares. | unit: `pixel-art.test.ts` (the layers make the whole square; the stencils' depths and turns; the coast shapes meet across quarters and squares; the shallows), `render.test.ts` (which cells of a square are whose, on fixtures named there; unexplored neighbours; how many pieces a square is drawn in). e2e: map baselines; the reference canvas still refuses anything off the grid or outside `ART_COLORS`. |
| 14 | Each colony's name is written under its square at the two nearest zooms (squares of 32 pixels and up), as pixel art and never canvas text: a 5 x 7 face of capitals, digits, space, hyphen, apostrophe and full stop (`LABEL_GLYPHS`), each letter white with an ink edge on every side, seven art pixels of the 32 grid apart, starting four in from the square's left edge on the first row below it. Accents are dropped, anything the face lacks is a question mark, and a name is cut at sixteen characters. Names are drawn over the pieces and under a dragged piece; a name that runs past the canvas is clipped, and a colony just above or left of the view still shows its name. Settlements carry no name; hidden-terrain view shows none. | unit: `pixel-art.test.ts` (the face, the edge, `labelText`), `render.test.ts` (place, zooms, clipping, the row above the view; map baseline). e2e: `visual.spec.ts` sheet and map baselines. |
| 15 | What stands on a square runs on into its like, and is laid in one order (R-1046). A forest joins any forest beside it but scrub, which stands alone; hills join hills and mountains mountains (`joins`, `joinMask` in `render.ts`, bits N, E, S, W on the square's look). For each such side the square's own picture is drawn once more under itself, moved half a square that way and cut off at the edge, so no row of bare ground is left between two of a kind. A river that runs out into the sea has a mouth on the water square (R-1047): the end piece of that river, in its own width, from that side to a pool in the middle of the square, with the pale rim as on land, which reads as foam (`mouthMask`, read from the land beside the water, its bits 16 to 128 saying which sides are a major river's). The mouth and the land square's own piece hold the same cells of the side they share. A neighbour that is off the map or unexplored neither joins nor has a mouth; the smallest zoom and the colony screen draw neither. The order of the over layer is: joins, the forest, hills or mountains, furrows, the river (with a forest cut back from it, point 3) or the mouths, the resource marker, the lost-city seal, the road, the totem. | unit: `pixel-art.test.ts` (what joins what; a join is the picture moved half a square and lies under the picture; the order; a mouth is the end piece of its river), `render.test.ts` (the masks on fixtures named there, unseen neighbours, the hidden-terrain view, no floor in the middle of the edge two forests share, the mouth from the edge to the pool in the middle, cell for cell with the river beside it, `lookKey` holds both masks). e2e: map baselines. |

Not drawn as pixel art: the sidebar, dialogs, reports and the colony screen's tokens are text
in the page's own fonts. Colours there are the page's, not the palette's. They come from the theme
tokens at the head of `src/ui/style.css` (parchment, ink, wood, brass, wax; `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`,
Theme), which the canvas never reads.
